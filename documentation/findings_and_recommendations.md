# Findings and Recommendations

## Findings

### Contract flexibility is strongly associated with churn

Month-to-Month customers recorded 45.84% churn, compared with 10.71% for One Year and 2.55% for Two Year contracts. Contract type is the clearest commercial segmentation variable in the analysis.

### Early tenure is the highest-risk stage

Customers with 0-12 months of tenure recorded 59.87% churn. The rate fell to 28.71% at 13-24 months, 20.39% at 25-48 months, and 9.51% after 48 months.

### Competitor pressure is the leading stated driver

Competitor-related reasons accounted for 841 churned customers, representing 45.00% of all churn. The two most frequent specific reasons were better devices and better offers from competitors.

### Service bundle depth is associated with retention

Among internet customers, churn ranged from 63.54% for customers with none of the four measured support add-ons to 5.32% for customers with all four. Tenure, contract, and customer needs may also influence this relationship, so the result should guide testing rather than be presented as causal proof.

### Historical value is concentrated among longer-tenure customers

Customers in the highest historical-revenue quartile recorded 14.44% churn, compared with 55.13% in the lowest quartile. Historical revenue naturally accumulates with tenure, so the result partly reflects the strong relationship between tenure and churn.

### Payment method and internet type identify useful segments

Mailed Check and Bank Withdrawal customers had churn rates of 36.88% and 34.00%. Credit Card customers recorded 14.48%. Fiber Optic customers had the highest internet-type churn at 40.72%.

## Recommendations

1. Introduce a structured first-year retention programme with check-ins after onboarding and before months 3, 6, and 12.
2. Test incentives that encourage suitable Month-to-Month customers to move to longer contracts without creating unfair switching barriers.
3. Review Fiber Optic reliability, support cases, pricing, and competitor comparisons before assuming the service technology itself causes churn.
4. Create competitor-response playbooks focused on device quality, plan value, and data allowances.
5. Test service bundles that combine security, backup, device protection, or technical support, while measuring incremental retention and margin.
6. Contact the 442 rule-based priority customers with tailored offers, then measure response and subsequent churn against a control group.
7. Investigate the concentrated San Diego ZIP-code patterns, while recognizing that the individual customer counts are small.

## Measurement plan

Future analysis should add customer-contact dates, offer acceptance, support incidents, network quality, plan changes, and monthly churn outcomes. Those fields would support cohort analysis, controlled retention testing, and predictive modelling.
